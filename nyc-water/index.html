Water Facts

What arrives at a New York tap, where it picks up what it carries, and what a system at home does about it. Every figure on this page has its source at the foot.

The journey

From the reservoir to your glass

The reservoirs

Nineteen reservoirs and three lakes across 2,000 square miles, as far as 125 miles north of the city, send a billion gallons a day. 1

The treatment

Chlorine, then ultraviolet light. Fluoride is added at 0.7 mg/L, and the pH is raised to slow corrosion in old pipes. About nine tenths of the supply is never filtered. 2 5

The mains

Seven thousand miles of aqueducts, tunnels and mains. The Delaware Aqueduct, the largest, has leaked for decades; its bypass is due to connect in 2027. A main break stirs rust and sediment down the line. 3 4

Your building

About 121,100 known lead or galvanized service lines remain, all privately owned, and the city does not test the plumbing inside a building. The last stretch is yours. 3

What a system removes

Five things a tap can carry

Pb

Lead. From service lines, and from the solder in older buildings. The federal health goal is zero, because no level is without risk. A reverse osmosis membrane removes it at the tap. 6

Cl

Chlorine. Added at the plant to keep the water safe on its way, and what you taste and smell when it arrives. Carbon takes it out. 2

PFAS

Forever chemicals. The federal limit for PFOA and PFOS is 4 parts per trillion, the lowest a laboratory can reliably measure. A reverse osmosis membrane is one of the few ways to remove them at home. 7

µm

Microplastics. Particles smaller than a micron pass most filters. A membrane with pores far smaller than that stops them.

NTU

Sediment. Rust and particles shaken loose by main breaks and repairs. If your water has ever run brown, this is why. A sediment stage catches it first. 3

Questions we are asked

About the water

Is New York water not already among the best in the country?
At the reservoir, yes, and the city tests it constantly. The question is everything after: a century of mains, the service line into the building, and the plumbing inside it, which the city does not test.
How do I know whether my building has a lead service line?
The city publishes a map of known service lines by address, and your landlord or managing agent will know what was replaced and when. A building from before 1961 is the likeliest to have one. A reverse osmosis system removes lead either way.
Do I need reverse osmosis, or is carbon enough?
Carbon takes out chlorine, taste and smell. Lead, PFAS, fluoride and microplastics need a membrane. The quiz asks about your building and what matters to you, and points to one or the other.
I rent. What can I do?
A countertop system connects to the tap without touching the plumbing and leaves with you when you move. An under-sink system is reversible too; many renters fit one and take it with them.
How often do the filters change?
It depends on the stage and on your water. The interval for each one is on its product page, and when a filter is due we say so.
